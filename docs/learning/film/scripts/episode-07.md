## On a real site

### 2. Paying for measured work

*On a real site · story*

On screen:

- Subcontractor · A specialist crew, paid for the work, not the days
- Work order · What, how much, at what rate: signed before they start
- Measurement · Our tape, on the finished work, with them present
- Held back · Retention 5% till the defects period; TDS 1% to the tax department
- Measured, not claimed. Held back, not forgotten.

Narration:

> A builder does not lay every tile with its own people. Tiling, waterproofing, painting, often go to a specialist crew: a subcontractor.
>
> First comes a work order: what they will do, how much, at what rate. Here, two hundred and forty square metres of vitrified floor at three hundred and twenty rupees, labour only.
>
> Then the work is measured, by our side, on the finished floor, with their person present. That number, not their bill, is what we owe for.
>
> And from each bill two things are held back. Retention, five percent, kept until the defects period ends, in case the tiles crack. And TDS, one percent, which the law says we pay to the tax department on their behalf.

## What can go wrong

### 10. Five ways a subcontract leaks

*What can go wrong · leaks*

On screen:

- Bill paid as sent · 92 billed, 81.88 laid
- Measured from paper · Their bill, not our tape
- Rate creep · Rs 340 on the bill, Rs 320 agreed
- Retention forgotten · Released early, or never
- TDS not paid on · Deducted, never deposited
- Our measurement, their rate, held back by the rule.

Narration:

> Five ways a subcontract leaks.
>
> Paying the bill as it comes, because the crew did work hard.
>
> Measuring from their bill, or from the drawing, instead of the finished floor.
>
> Paying a rate that is not the work order's.
>
> And forgetting what was held: retention never released, or released before the defects period ends; TDS deducted and never paid to the tax department.
