## On a real site

### 2. From drawings to a signed contract

*Before day 1 · timeline*

On screen:

- Drawings · Architect and structural consultant: what to build
- Quantities · Measured off the drawings, line by line: the BOQ
- Rates · Client's rate, and your labour and material cost
- Quote · The price the client compares and accepts
- Contract · Price, dates, advance, retention, defects, delay

Narration:

> Every job starts as drawings. The architect draws what to build. The structural consultant sizes the footings, the columns and the steel.
>
> From the drawings, the builder measures everything: so many cubic metres of excavation, so many square metres of plastering. That list is the bill of quantities: the BOQ.
>
> Each line gets two prices. What the client will pay for one unit. And what it will cost the builder: the labour and the material.
>
> The builder quotes, the client compares, and they agree.
>
> Then the contract: the price, the dates, and the rules for how money moves for the next nine months.

### 4. The BOQ is the yardstick for everything after

*Why it matters · story*

On screen:

- A day on site · Ep 3 · Each piece of work reported lands on its line
- Material · Ep 4 · What each line needs: cement, sand, steel
- Wages · Ep 5 · Labour paid against the work it earned
- Subcontract · Ep 7 · The tiler measured and paid on his lines
- Billing · Ep 8 · Each month: quantity done × client rate
- Making money? · Ep 9 · Earned against spent, line by line
- A wrong BOQ makes every later number wrong.

Narration:

> Here is why the BOQ matters so much. It is not a document you file away. Everything after day one is measured against it.
>
> Every piece of work the site reports lands on a BOQ line, so you know how far each line has got.
>
> The material each line needs comes from it. So does the check on whether the labour was worth the work it did.
>
> A subcontractor is measured and paid on its lines. And every month, the client is billed for the quantity done on each line, at its rate.
>
> And at any moment, comparing what each line earned with what it really cost tells you if the job is making money.
>
> Get the BOQ wrong, and every one of these is wrong too.

### 5. What the contract adds

*The contract, GV/2026/007 · cards*

On screen:

- Advance 10% · ₹8,20,605 at the start; 10% of each bill taken back
- Retention 5% · Held from each bill until 12 months after handover
- Monthly bills · Measured work × BOQ rates; paid within 15 days
- ₹25,000 a week · For each week past 31 July 2027

Narration:

> The contract adds the rules for money and time. Eighty-two lakh rupees for the work, with GST at eighteen percent on top.
>
> A mobilisation advance: ten percent, about eight lakh twenty thousand, paid at the start so the builder can set up the site. It is taken back, ten percent from every bill, until it is all recovered.
>
> Retention: five percent of every bill is held back by the client, and released only when the defects period ends, twelve months after handover.
>
> Bills every month, on the work measured, at the BOQ rates, paid within fifteen days.
>
> And handover on the thirty-first of July. Every week late costs twenty-five thousand rupees.

### 6. ₹10 lakh of work: what reaches the bank

*What the terms do to cash · stack*

On screen:

- 8,50,000 · To spend this month
- 1,00,000 · Advance taken back
- 50,000 · Retention
- ₹8.5 L, not ₹10 L · Plan cash on what reaches the bank
- GST ₹1,80,000 is added on top and passed to the government. Green Villa's client is a person, so no TDS.

Narration:

> See what those terms do to cash. Say the site does ten lakh rupees of work in a month, at the BOQ rates.
>
> One lakh is taken back against the advance the builder already received.
>
> Fifty thousand is held as retention, for well over a year.
>
> So eight and a half lakh is what the company gets to spend this month. GST of one lakh eighty thousand comes in on top, and goes straight on to the government.
>
> A builder who plans on the full ten lakh will run short, every single month.

## What can go wrong

### 13. Five ways a job is lost on paper

*What can go wrong · leaks*

On screen:

- Priced below cost · Staircase: -₹300 a cum
- Lines with no cost · Tiling: margin looks bigger
- Work with no line · Putty and primer: never billed
- Quantity copied wrong · Bedroom-2: 163 sq ft printed, ~140 drawn
- Terms unread · Advance, retention, ₹25,000 a week late
- Each one is fixed for free on paper, and paid for on site.

Narration:

> Five ways a job is lost before the first brick.
>
> A line priced below its cost, like the staircase. And lines with no cost at all, which make the margin look bigger than it is.
>
> Work with no line. Green Villa's estimate has no line for putty and primer. The site will do it, and it can never be billed.
>
> A quantity copied wrong from the drawings. Bedroom two is printed as a hundred and sixty-three square feet. Drawn, it is about a hundred and forty.
>
> And contract terms nobody reads: the advance coming back out of every bill, the retention, and twenty-five thousand rupees for every week late.
