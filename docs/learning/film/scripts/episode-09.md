## On a real site

### 2. How a builder knows

*On a real site · story*

On screen:

- The yardstick · The BOQ: what a unit should cost, and what it earns
- Earned · Work done × the line's rates
- Spent · The crew and the material that went into it
- Books vs real · Billed against paid, or earned against spent
- Nobody types a total. The totals come from the entries.

Narration:

> The BOQ is the yardstick. Each line says what a unit of work should cost: labour, material, and what the client pays for it.
>
> So every piece of work done earns its share of the budget. Plaster forty square metres, and the line has earned forty times its rates.
>
> Against that, what was really spent: the crew that day, the cement that went into it. Earned against spent, line by line, is the real margin.
>
> The books are different. They count bills: what was billed to the client against what was paid out. Work done and not billed yet makes the books look worse than the job. Both are true; they answer different questions.

## What can go wrong

### 9. Five ways the numbers lie

*What can go wrong · leaks*

On screen:

- Not on a line · Spent, and never judged
- Books read as real · Not billed yet ≠ a loss
- Too early to tell · Day 5: '655% over'
- Seen too late · A month of rework
- Stock read as cost · Cement in the store is not spent
- Enter it once, on its line. Then every page can be trusted.

Narration:

> Five ways the numbers lie.
>
> Work and labour on no BOQ line: cost control cannot see them, so a line looks fine while the money is going somewhere nobody looks.
>
> Reading the books as the real margin: a job that is fine, looking like a loss, because it was not billed.
>
> A forecast from three days of work. Mesiri waits until a tenth of the budget is earned.
>
> And noticing at the month's end what Green / red shows the same day.
